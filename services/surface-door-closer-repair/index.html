Surface-mounted closers are the ones you can see above the door, and they are the bulk of our Vaughan calls. Three failures cover almost all of them: the body has lost hydraulic fluid and the door either slams or has no control at all; the arm, forearm or shoe bracket has torn loose from a door that was shouldered or blown open past its limit; or the unit was undersized for the opening it was hung on and has been fighting a losing battle ever since.

A closer is a sealed hydraulic unit. There is no fill port, so a leaking body cannot be topped up, and backing a valve out to chase more speed on a dry unit is how the rest of the fluid ends up on the floor. What we can do is tell you honestly whether the closer is worth adjusting or has to come off the wall, size the replacement properly for the door width, weight and exposure, and reinforce the mounting so the next gust across an open Highway 400 business park does not tear the arm out of the frame again.

Most of these are single-visit jobs. We carry common surface closers, arms, brackets, drop plates and templates on the truck, so a slamming door in a Concord warehouse office or a Woodbridge plaza unit usually gets sorted the same day rather than being wedged open until a part arrives.

FAQs

Surface Door Closer Repair & Replacement — your questions

My door closer is leaking oil — can it be refilled?

No. Modern closers are sealed hydraulic bodies with no fill port, so once the fluid is out the unit has to be replaced. A leaking closer either slams hard enough to split the frame or stalls short of the latch, and on a fire door that second failure is a Fire Code problem, not just a nuisance.

The door slams. Can you just turn a screw and fix it?

Sometimes, yes — sweep speed and latch speed are separate valves, and a door that races only over the last few centimetres is often just a latch-speed adjustment. But if the body is dry or a valve has already been backed out too far, adjusting further makes it worse. We check the unit first and tell you which one you are actually dealing with.

How long should a commercial door closer last in Vaughan?

A properly sized unit on a normal commercial entrance lasts many years. What shortens it here is cycle count, impact and weather: a shipping-door man-door in a Concord warehouse or a street entrance taking salt, grit and freeze-thaw wears far faster than an interior office door, and an undersized closer on a heavy exterior door fails early no matter whose name is on it.
