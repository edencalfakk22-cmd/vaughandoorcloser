On an industrial site, closer adjustment is a scheduling problem more than a technical one. The units drift out of tune because of how hard the doors work, so the useful question is not which door is broken — it is when anyone last walked all of them.

What shifts them: cold thickening the hydraulic fluid until a door that latched in September stalls short of the strike; summer humidity swelling leaves and gaskets so a door starts binding; wind loading on entrances facing open parking; and sheer cycle count on the dock and plant doors, which loosens everything bolted on. All four are predictable and all four are seasonal.

A pass across a site takes one visit. We check each unit for fluid loss and for arm or bracket damage, set sweep speed, latch speed and backcheck where the unit allows it, release every rated door from full open to confirm it latches unaided, and measure opening force on barrier-free routes — the Building Code limits that, and winding a spring up to beat a draft is how the limit gets broken without anyone meaning to. Your facilities team gets one written list: what was adjusted, what needs parts, and which units will not see another winter.

FAQs

Closing-Speed Adjustment & Winter Tune-Up — your questions

Can a whole industrial site be done in one visit?

That is the way it should be done. We walk every opening on the site — dock offices, plant-to-office separations, stairs, service rooms, the main entrance — adjust what will adjust, flag the units that have lost fluid, verify the rated doors latch, and hand your facilities team one written list. It is a fraction of the disruption of a dozen separate call-outs.

Our doors open onto a huge parking lot and the wind wrecks them. What helps?

Backcheck, mostly, plus enough spring power to close against the wind without making the door unusable. Backcheck slows the door near the end of the opening swing so a gust cannot fling it into the wall and take the arm, the bracket or the glass beside it with it. On exposed frontages it is the single most valuable setting on the closer.

The closers on our dock doors go out of adjustment constantly. Is that normal?

For that traffic, yes — a dock-office door can see more cycles in a month than an office door sees in years, and everything loosens. It is also a hint that the units may be undersized for the opening. We check whether adjustment is really the answer, or whether that door has been running on the wrong closer for years.
