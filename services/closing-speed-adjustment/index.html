Closers are not a set-and-forget item in this climate. Between the cold thickening the hydraulic fluid, the summer humidity swelling doors, and the pressure differences a tall building at the Vaughan Metropolitan Centre generates in January, the same unit wants different settings in winter than it does in July. Most of the failures we get called out for mid-cold-snap were visible weeks earlier as a door that was closing a little slower every day.

A tune-up visit is straightforward. We walk the doors, check each closer for fluid loss and arm or bracket damage, set sweep speed, latch speed and backcheck where the unit allows it, confirm every fire door still latches from any open position, and check opening force on barrier-free entrances so a door that was made heavier to beat a draft has not quietly become one nobody can pull open. The Building Code limits opening force on doors in a barrier-free path of travel, and cranking a spring up is the usual way that limit gets broken.

Buildings with a lot of openings — apartment and condo towers, schools, plazas, distribution buildings with an office block on the front — get far more out of one scheduled pass than out of six separate emergency call-outs. We book the pre-winter round from early autumn across Vaughan and leave a written list of what was adjusted and what needs replacing before the cold does it for us.

FAQs

Closing-Speed Adjustment & Winter Tune-Up — your questions

How often should commercial door closers be checked?

Twice a year suits most Vaughan buildings — once before the cold and once after it — with fire doors verified more often, because the Fire Code expects their self-closing devices to be kept in operating condition, not merely to have worked once when they were installed.

Can our maintenance staff adjust the closers themselves?

The speed valves look simple, but sweep, latch and backcheck each control a different part of the swing, and backing one out too far dumps the remaining fluid onto the floor. If someone in-house is going to touch them, we will show them which valve does what while we are on site.

Why do all our doors get worse at the same time in winter?

Because the same two things hit them at once: colder fluid moving more slowly through the valves, and stack pressure inside the building pushing and pulling on the doors. It is normal, it is predictable, and it is exactly why a seasonal adjustment round is worth booking before the first deep freeze.
