When you cannot see a closer on a Vaughan entrance, it is either concealed in the door or the transom above it, or it is a floor spring buried in the slab under the pivot. Both show up constantly on the aluminum and all-glass entrances around the Vaughan Metropolitan Centre, in Thornhill and Woodbridge professional buildings, and across the office fronts stitched onto the front of industrial units all through the Highway 400 and 407 employment lands, where nobody wanted a box hanging over the door.

Floor springs are the ones our winter punishes hardest. The cement case sits at grade, exactly where meltwater, brine and grit get tracked in, and once the cover-plate seal gives up the unit corrodes from the inside out. The symptoms are a door that no longer self-centres, a hold-open detent that has stopped working, oil weeping around the plate, or a leaf that has visibly dropped because the bottom pivot inside the spring has worn.

These are more involved than a surface swap — a floor spring means lifting the door off, opening the case and often re-shimming and re-aligning the whole leaf on its pivots. We do that work, replace concealed overhead closers in aluminum stiles and transoms, and when a floor-spring case is genuinely beyond saving we say so plainly and lay out the alternatives instead of burying labour in a unit that will fail again next February.

FAQs

Concealed & Floor-Spring Closers — your questions

Why did our floor spring fail after only a few winters?

Salt and meltwater. The case sits at grade in the traffic path, so brine and grit are carried straight onto it every time the door opens in February. Once the seal at the cover plate lets go, corrosion and contamination inside the case do the rest — it is the single most common way floor springs die in this climate.

Can a concealed closer be replaced without a new door?

Usually yes. Overhead concealed units in an aluminum stile or transom can normally be swapped into the existing prep, and we template to match. If the prep was cut for a discontinued model we confirm what fits before anything comes off the frame.

Our glass entrance door has dropped and drags. Is that the closer?

Often it is the pivot inside the floor spring rather than the closing mechanism itself. A worn bottom pivot lets the leaf sag, which is why it drags on the threshold and stops latching. We check the bottom pivot, the top centre and the spring together rather than adjusting valves and hoping.
