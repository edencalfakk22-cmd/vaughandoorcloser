The office block bolted onto the front of a warehouse almost always has a glass entrance, and that entrance almost always has its closer hidden — in the transom, in the door stile, or in a cement case in the floor. Same at the showroom fronts along Highway 7 and around the Vaughan Metropolitan Centre. It looks better and it is harder to service, which is a trade nobody who signs the lease is ever consulted on.

Two things kill these units here. The first is the winter: a floor-spring case sits at grade in the traffic path, and once the cover-plate seal gives up, meltwater, brine and grit corrode the mechanism from the inside. The second is wind. A vestibule door facing a hectare of open parking gets flung to its stop by a gust, and if backcheck has failed or was never set, the arm, the stop and sometimes the glass beside it take the impact.

Servicing one means lifting the leaf off, opening the case, and usually re-shimming and re-aligning the door on its pivots afterwards — a different job from swapping a surface unit, and one worth doing properly. Where the cement case is sound, the mechanism comes out and a matching one goes in with no concrete work. Where it has gone, we say so before the door is off and lay out the options rather than putting labour into something that fails again next February.

FAQs

Floor-Spring & Concealed Closer Service — your questions

Our showroom entrance door swings past the stop and hits the wall. Why?

Backcheck — the part of the closer that slows the door near the end of the opening swing — has failed, or was never set. On a big-box or showroom frontage with wind straight off the parking lot, backcheck is what protects the door, the hardware and the glass beside it. On a floor spring it is a mechanism inside the case, so it is a lift-the-door job rather than a valve tweak.

Do concealed closers cope with heavy commercial traffic?

The right one does, but the specification matters more than it does for a surface unit, because the mechanism is hidden and nobody notices it struggling until the door stops behaving. We size for leaf weight and the cycle count the entrance actually takes — which for a retail entrance off Highway 7 is a very different number from an office lobby.

The cover plate on our floor closer is loose and rattling. Is that urgent?

It is worth dealing with quickly. A loose plate lets water, salt and grit straight into the case, which is what corrodes the mechanism from the inside, and it is a trip hazard in a public entrance. Re-seating the plate is a small job next to replacing the unit it is supposed to be protecting.
