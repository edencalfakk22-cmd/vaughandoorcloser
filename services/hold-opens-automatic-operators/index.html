Ask a facilities manager which of their doors are held open and they will name three. Walk the building and you will usually find seven, and two of them are being held by something that will never release.

That is the whole compliance question, and it has a simple test: on an alarm signal, does the device let go, and does the door then travel all the way to a latched position on its own? A magnetic wall holder wired to the panel passes. A closer with an integral electromagnetic hold-open passes. A wedge, a hook, a length of strapping, a pallet and a mechanical hold-open arm all fail, whatever the door looks like while it is open.

Power operators are the other side of this work — the push-plate units on entrances, accessible washrooms and suites. In a plant they matter for a reason people miss: when the power drops or a board fails, the door falls back to whatever the closer behind it is set at, and on an industrial entrance that closer has often been wound up hard. An accessible entrance that only works while the motor runs is not an accessible entrance. We service the holders, alarm interfaces, plates, actuators, arms and control boxes, and set the manual behaviour of the closer deliberately.

FAQs

Hold-Open Magnets & Push-Plate Operators — your questions

Our cross-corridor doors are held open with a magnet. How do we know it is wired properly?

Test it, and record the result. On an alarm signal the magnet should let go and the closer should take the door all the way to a latched position, from wherever it was being held. If the door drops but does not latch, you have half a working system. We test the release as part of the visit and note which doors passed.

Can a power operator be fitted to a door that has a closer already?

In most cases yes — low-energy operators are commonly retrofitted, and the existing closer is either incorporated or replaced as part of the work. What has to be checked first is the door weight, the swing, the power supply and whether the frame will carry the operator. We look at all four before quoting rather than after.

Our plant has an accessible entrance nobody can use when the power is out. Is that normal?

Normal, but not acceptable. When the operator is dead the door falls back to the closer, so if that closer is set hard the entrance becomes unusable for exactly the people the operator was installed for. We set the manual opening force deliberately, so the door still works with or without power.
