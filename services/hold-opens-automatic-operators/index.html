There are two legitimate ways to keep a door open in a Vaughan building, and neither of them is a wedge. The first is a hold-open device — a magnetic holder, or a closer with an integral electromagnetic hold-open — wired so it releases the door on a fire alarm signal. That is how a corridor or vestibule door in a Thornhill apartment tower, a Maple long-term care home or a warehouse-to-office separation in Concord can stand open for traffic all day and still close when it matters.

The second is a low-energy power operator: the push-plate operators on barrier-free entrances, washrooms and accessible suites. Those are a genuine accessibility feature, and when the operator dies the door reverts to whatever the closer behind it is set at — which is exactly when an entrance that was fine in October becomes unusable for someone in a chair in January.

We install and service both: magnetic holders and alarm interfaces, closer-holder combination units, push plates, wireless actuators, operator arms and control boxes, and the closer behind the operator so the door still behaves sensibly when the power is out or the unit is waiting on a part.

FAQs

Hold-Opens & Automatic Door Operators — your questions

Is a magnetic hold-open allowed on a fire door?

Yes, when it is tied into the fire alarm so the door releases and self-closes on an alarm signal. That is the compliant alternative to propping. What is never allowed is a doorstop, a wedge or a hook holding a fire door open with nothing to release it.

Our accessible push-plate operator stopped working. Can it be repaired?

Usually. Most failures are the actuator, the control board, a wiring fault or a worn operator arm rather than the whole unit. We diagnose on site and repair where parts are still available, replacing only when it genuinely makes sense.

What happens to the automatic door in a power failure?

It falls back to the closer behind it, which is why that closer has to be set correctly rather than left slack because the operator normally does the work. We set the manual behaviour deliberately so the door still closes and latches when the operator is dead.
