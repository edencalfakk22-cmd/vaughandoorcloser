An exit device has one non-negotiable job: it releases the door in a single motion from the inside, with no key, no thumbturn and no special knowledge. Ontario's Building Code calls for panic hardware on exit doors serving an assembly floor area with an occupant load over one hundred, which covers a great deal of Vaughan — banquet halls and event venues along Highway 7 and Weston Road, Woodbridge restaurants, places of worship, school gyms, arenas and community centres, plus the assembly and retail space folded into the Vaughan Mills and Interchange Way area.

The failures are predictable. Vertical-rod devices on double doors fall out of adjustment and the top latch stops engaging. Rim devices get dogged down permanently so the door never latches at all. Touch bars get bent by carts and skids in warehouse and back-of-house exits, latches wear round and start slipping, and outside trim seizes after a winter of salt and grit. On fire-rated openings the device must not be dogged, and labelled fire exit hardware has to stay labelled — we will not substitute non-rated parts onto a rated assembly.

We repair and adjust rim, mortise and surface vertical-rod devices, replace worn latches, dogging mechanisms and outside trim, sort out electric strikes and delayed-egress arrangements where they exist, and pair the device with a correctly set closer so the door latches cleanly instead of bouncing off the strike.

FAQs

Panic Bars & Exit Device Service — your questions

Which Vaughan doors actually need panic hardware?

The Building Code requires it on exit doors serving an assembly floor area with an occupant load over one hundred — banquet halls, restaurants, places of worship, gyms and similar. Plenty of other commercial and industrial exits carry exit devices by choice, because they are the most reliable way to guarantee one-motion egress under load.

Our exit door will not open from the inside. How fast can you come?

That is a life-safety emergency and we dispatch it as a priority. Do not chain, block or re-route people past it in the meantime — station someone at the door and call us. We will get a technician out and get the opening releasing properly again.

Can we dog the panic bar down so the door stays unlatched during business hours?

On a non-rated door, mechanical dogging is a normal feature and we will set it up properly. On a fire-rated door, no — fire exit hardware must not be dogged, because the door has to latch to hold the separation. If you need it to stand open, a hold-open tied to the fire alarm is the right answer.
